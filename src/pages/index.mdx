## Abstract
`PolyPose` is a simple and robust algorithm for localizing deformable 3D anatomy from sparse 2D X-ray images. Think of it as **3D GPS for image-guided interventions,** enabling clinicians or surgical robotic systems to know exactly where critical structures are in 3D from routine 2D X-rays acquired during intraoperative navigation.

Deformable 2D/3D registration is a long-standing and difficult problem. The central challenge is that soft tissues have very poor contrast on X-ray, making their complex 3D deformations nearly impossible to estimate from sparse 2D X-rays. `PolyPose` overcomes this limitation by parameterizing complex 3D deformation fields as a composition of rigid transforms, exploiting the biologic constraint that **bones do not bend in typical motion.** This yields a low-rank model of human motion that is far easier to optimize while also being highly expressive.

Enforcing anatomically plausible priors by construction enables `PolyPose` to successfully align a patient's preoperative volume to **as few as two X-rays.** Across extensive experiments on diverse datasets from orthopedic surgery and radiotherapy, `PolyPose` provides accurate 3D alignment in challenging sparse-view and limited-angle settings where current registration methods fail.

</HighlightedSection>

## Future Work

There are many interesting extensions that could be made to `PolyPose`! For example, one could **enfore skeleton constraints,** requiring that all rigid bodies connected at oints influence each other via a kinematic chain. As demonstrated by the initial implementation in `PolyPose`, enforcing physics-based priors makes models more robust in sparse-data settings. Additionally, the warped CTs produced by the estimated deformation fields could be used to initialize **_post-hoc_ 3D image reconstruction** techniques (e.g., using differentiable rendering techniques such as [`DiffVox`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19224)). This approach could be used to reconstruct changes from the preoperative to the intraoperative state in 3D (e.g., implanted devices, altered morphology, etc.).
